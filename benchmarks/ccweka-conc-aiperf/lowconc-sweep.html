KV-offload benchmark · patched vLLM 0.30

CC-weka Low-Concurrency Sweep

Certus (SPDK NVMe tier) against cputier (vLLM's native CPU + filesystem tier) on the 1-output-token CC-weka trace, replayed with each turn's recorded think time. Every arm ran its full hour on the patched scheduler, and none stalled.

  • Model Qwen2.5-7B-1M dense @ 262k
  • GPU 1, KV pool 359,952 tokens
  • DRAM tier 64 GB each
  • Arm 3600 s
  • Arms run 20, 0 stalls
  • Certus
  • cputier
Hover or tab through a chart to compare both backends at one cap.

What the sweep shows

  • The gap grows with concurrency.Certus leads on request rate at every cap: +8% at 4 streams, +30% at 16, and +47–57% at 32 and 64.
  • Certus throughput is nearly flat.It stays at 0.56–0.65 req/s from 4 to 64 streams. cputier falls from 0.60 to about 0.38 and levels off from 32 streams.
  • cputier's tier stops keeping up.Its hit rate drops from 93% to 83% and the share of tokens it recomputes rises from 6% to 17%. Certus stays at or above 90%.

Notes

TTFT here is effectively the whole request, since every turn generates one token. It is mostly time queued in vLLM: it tracks in-flight cap ÷ request rate (Little's law), because one GPU serves about one long request at a time.

"Errored" counts client-side connection-reuse failures (ServerDisconnectedError / ClientOSError) that failed within 5 ms of sending and never reached vLLM. Each row is the run with the fewest errors; duplicate runs agree within about 3%.

All arms ran with a scheduler patch that fixes a deadlock in stock vLLM 0.30, where requests whose KV load had finished could wait forever behind a request that could not be admitted. Unpatched, both backends wedged at 8 and 16 streams and cputier also at 32 and 64. Combined table: /mnt/certus1/vllm-serve-matrix/ccweka-lowconc-patched-combined.md.
